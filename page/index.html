    How the calls work

    My value is what I think one share is worth today: the average of a bad, a middle, and a good future for the business, weighted by how likely each is. I set it before looking at the market price. Every stock gets the same rule. If the price is 20% or more below my value, the call is Buy. If it is 20% or more above, the call is Sell. In between, the call is Hold. Confidence is shown, but it does not change the rule. “Underpriced” and “overpriced” are the price’s gap to my value, recomputed at every price refresh. Return at today’s price is the yearly return a buyer earns holding from today’s price if my forecast comes true. Funds get a review but no call. Dividend yield is the last 12 months of dividends divided by the latest price. What to do in each account is in the monthly meeting.

    Prices refresh on the schedule you set, and on request. My values change in the monthly review, or early after an earnings report or when a price crosses a buy or sell line. This is analysis for the owner’s own decisions, not licensed financial advice.
